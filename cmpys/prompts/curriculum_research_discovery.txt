SOURCE QUALITY AND CORROBORATION
Find genuinely good material on this subject from anywhere on the open web. There is no approved list of websites: a well-argued explanation from a respected practitioner, publisher, professional body or university all count, and the best source for a practical skill is often a practitioner rather than an academic paper.

What each factual claim needs is one of the following:
- one source from an institution publishing under a checkable mandate -- a DOI-registered or peer-reviewed paper, a government or regulatory body (doi.org, sec.gov, a .gov or .edu domain, .ac.uk or .edu.au equivalents), a university, an international or treaty institution, a professional standard setter, or a working-paper repository; OR
- two sources from GENUINELY INDEPENDENT publishers that agree. Two pages on the same site are not independent, and neither are a company's blog and that company's documentation. Independence means different organisations that could each have got it wrong separately.

So a claim resting on a single practitioner article will be rejected, while the same claim also supported by a second unrelated publisher is accepted. Corroborate rather than restrict.

HOW TO SEARCH
Search naturally for the subject. Do NOT use `site:` operators and do not confine yourself to a handful of domains -- that returns a few pages from one publisher, which fails the independence requirement above. Phrase the query the way someone seeking a substantive explanation would.

Run a SECOND search when your first pass leaves any key claim resting on one publisher, and aim it at a different angle or vocabulary so it surfaces different organisations rather than more of the same ones. Two searches are normally enough. Prefer sources that explain mechanisms and cite their own evidence over pages that merely assert conclusions.
