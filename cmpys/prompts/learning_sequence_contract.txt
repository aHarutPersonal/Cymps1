LEARNING SEQUENCE — express these decisions through the existing output fields:
- Each description names one observable learning demand, the evidence the learner
  will produce and a quality check. A familiar prerequisite can be practiced in a
  new decision without being re-taught as a new introductory topic.
- Structure the sequence as an explicit cognitive progression:
  (1) Step 1 (Diagnostic + Quick Win): address provisional placement with one short
      goal-specific check and a targeted explanation within the existing time. Use
      the supplied diagnostic response and criterion; explicitly excerpted work
      cannot establish omitted reasoning. If the target needs confirmation, help
      define a small observable result before substantial practice; do not invent
      the learner's consent.
  (2) Core Steps (Worked-Example Fading): reduce procedural hints in the learner
      exercise as prerequisites develop. Every tutorial still includes its required
      reading worked example on a distinct public case. Never remove that example
      or reuse its solution as the supposedly independent assessment.
  (3) Interleaved Discrimination Checkpoints (every 4–5 steps where the sequence
      introduces related methods): include a mixed case requiring the learner to
      choose the method and explain the deciding cue. Use only supplied prerequisites
      or methods explicitly taught earlier in this sequence; do not invent mastery.
  (4) Final Step (Capstone Transfer): check the mission's outcome on a self-contained
      new case—produce or justify a small artifact or decision combining the relevant
      taught skills, with observable criteria and reduced procedural help. Keep one
      primary objective and the allocated time; isolated quiz completion is not the outcome.
- Use a brief reconstruction, prediction or self-explanation where it supports
  the goal. Distribute these checks through the sequence, not every technique in
  every lesson. A difficult question is useful only if its prerequisites are met.
- Activities are authored separately. Supply the needed case and source material
  within each activity; do not assume access to a learner's earlier answer or an
  evolving saved artifact unless that input is explicitly supplied. Use a complete
  new case for synthesis when cross-lesson artifact handoff is unavailable.
- Allocate review and synthesis inside the mission budget. Only an actual elapsed
  interval establishes spacing; short lessons or sequential unlocks do not. Do not
  promise automatic delayed checks, notifications or unsupported response controls.
