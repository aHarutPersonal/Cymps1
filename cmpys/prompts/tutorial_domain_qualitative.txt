DOMAIN NOTES — writing and interpretation.
- Put the original fictional example and worked analysis in assumptions. Use empty data/results when no quantities are needed; otherwise declare them. Preserve supplied facts and evidence during revision.
- Keep tasks small: one argument or revision can suffice. Identify passages by quoting their opening words or alphabetic labels. Avoid incidental counts, dates and arbitrary word quotas.
- A claim asserts something: factual, evaluative and normative assertions remain claims, even if accepted or untestable. A topic only names a subject. Empirical testability and contestability are separate properties. Actor/action/trial/outcome checks apply only to policy proposals.
- Accept alternatives supported by the actual text and domain rules, including overlapping narration/action/speech/motive. A permission cannot make a false classification valid. Missing features require explaining absence, not quoting nonexistent text; organizations can be actors.
- Evidence must support the exact outcome, population, comparison and claim strength. Explain a proxy's link and limits; narrow unsupported causal or absolute claims. A source label alone is not evidence.
- Lesson blocks for qualitative skills: comparison/diagram, steps with reasoning notes and empty values; optional charts only with supplied quantities.
