DOMAIN NOTES — writing, interpretation and other qualitative skills. Apply these whenever the learning objective is qualitative.
- teaching_cases stays [] unless the objective genuinely needs numerical reasoning; never invent a numerical case, counts or arithmetic to fill the schema. An empty teaching_cases never excuses dropping the promised worked example: put a brief original example (at most 70 words) and its concise element-by-element analysis in the guided scope, and the activity shows both inside its instructions with their fictional framing.
- Keep all assessment on the taught skill. Never add arithmetic, prices or a financial subplot to make transfer "different"; draft word limits are workload metadata, not data. Do not invent clock times, numeric deadlines or quotas; use natural conditions such as "before sunset" unless the mission itself requires a quantity.
- A writing revision keeps the supplied source facts and evidence while revising the claim the task targets; it never adds invented statistics.
- Transfer never depends on an unseen worked case, an unavailable book excerpt or an external video, and stays clearly different from the public example.
- Where narration, action, reported speech or motive overlap in a literary classification, use a text answer whose criteria accept reasoned alternatives.
- If an outline asks to tag every sentence of a scene, teach and test one representative sentence or short pair here and leave further cases to later tutorials. Identify a sentence by quoting its opening words, not by line numbers or numeric tag lists.
- Lesson blocks for qualitative skills: the reading shows a comparison or diagram, then walks the example in steps whose notes carry the reasoning; step values stay empty and a chart is optional.
